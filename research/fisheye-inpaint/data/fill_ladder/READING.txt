================================================================
This answers the question the whole investigation was for. Quote the
FITTED-LIGHTING ladder; the historical one is kept because the two disagree,
which is itself the finding that justified running both.

ANSWER: a generative filler is not worth building.

  On the calibrated render, `replicate` -- nearest-valid smear, which invents
  NOTHING -- already captures most or all of what true content is worth:

    cell              oracle   replicate   remaining   replicate %
    single  fisheye   0.0152     0.0126      0.0026        82.9%
    single  persp     0.0174     0.0135      0.0039        77.6%
    8-frame fisheye   0.0176     0.0169      0.0007        96.0%
    8-frame persp     0.0102     0.0129     -0.0027       126.5%

  The budget left for ANY generative method is 0.000 to 0.004 AbsRel. In the
  8-frame perspective cell a smear BEATS the rendered truth: the true corner
  content is complex and the smear is not, and the model prefers the smear.

THE RATIO DID NOT SURVIVE THE LIGHTING CHANGE -- which is why both were run.
  replicate's share, historical -> fitted:
    single  fisheye   23.9% -> 82.9%
    single  persp     37.8% -> 77.6%
    8-frame fisheye   38.0% -> 96.0%
    8-frame persp     84.7% -> 126.5%
  On the badly-lit render the conclusion would have been "cheap fills capture
  only 24-85%, so there is room". There is not. A percentage is a ratio and its
  denominator moved 4-6x; assuming it would cancel would have been wrong.

AN UNEXPECTED ASYMMETRY, CONSISTENT ACROSS BOTH RENDERS
  A flat mean fill HURTS in the fisheye domain and HELPS in the perspective one:
                       single fisheye   single persp
    mean                    -225%           +68%
    chanmean                -228%           +61%
  (historical render: -64%/-58% fisheye, +7%/+11% persp -- same signs)

  This bears directly on the remembered claim that "mean fill beats black",
  which five literature searches failed to source. On a RECTIFIED image it holds.
  On a RAW FISHEYE it is not merely absent, it is much worse than black. The
  claim is domain-dependent and must not be carried across.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE ORIGINAL IDEA
  Ground-truth content in the invalid region is worth 0.010-0.018 AbsRel; a fill
  that invents nothing takes 77-126% of it; a generative filler is left with
  0-0.004. And the effect does not depend on the projection, so the premise --
  that completion helps by returning the image to the perspective training
  domain -- has no support. It is a low-level "do not feed the model large black
  regions" effect.
