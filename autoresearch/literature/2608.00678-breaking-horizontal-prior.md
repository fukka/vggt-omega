# Breaking the Horizontal Prior: From Long-Tailed Orientation Bias to Roll-Robust Monocular Depth Estimation

- arXiv: 2608.00678 (2026-08) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00678
- Read: 2026-09-07, via abstract + HTML.

## Claim

Depth foundation models carry a **horizontal prior**: training images are
overwhelmingly captured near-level (human photographic habit), so the models
degrade under camera roll. Confirmed on Marigold, GenPercept, Depth Anything V2,
Distill Any Depth (DINOv2 ViT-L encoders).

## Their numbers (Tab. 2, DAv2), roll sampled UNIFORMLY in a range

| setting | roll range | AbsRel | delta_1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Horizontal | 0 deg | 0.096 | 0.922 |
| Shaking | [0,15] | 0.109 | 0.906 |
| Rolling | [0,45] | 0.119 | 0.895 |
| Tipping | [0,90] | 0.127 | 0.883 |

+32% relative AbsRel from horizontal to tipping. Datasets: DIODE, ScanNet,
ETH3D, KITTI, NYUv2 — **all perspective, none egocentric, none fisheye**.

## Their fix

**ID-Constraint**: a *training-time* regulariser. Fine-tune the depth backbone
jointly with six rotation-invariant auxiliary tasks (region level: light/shadow,
occlusion, relative size, texture gradient; pixel level: local peak, local
slope). Auxiliary heads are discarded at inference, so the deployed
architecture is unchanged. Trained on 200k SA-1B images + DepthCues subsets.

## What they do NOT do — the gap this project sits in

1. **No gravity/IMU input.** They note commercial systems "typically rely on
   auxiliary hardware sensors (e.g. six-axis IMU)" and deliberately develop a
   purely algorithmic path instead.
2. Their best **algorithmic roll predictor has 25.9 deg average error**. That is
   a strong argument for using an IMU when the platform has one — and every
   egocentric device does.
3. No fisheye, no egocentric data, and no measurement of what the roll
   distribution actually IS on a real head-mounted camera. Their roll settings
   are synthetic uniform draws.

## How it lines up with our numbers

Ours (h16 `roll_controls.py`, DA3-Small, clean pinhole protocol, FIXED angle
rather than a uniform draw, seq136 20 frames):
0 deg 0.1545 -> 20 deg +13% -> 30 deg +46% -> 40 deg +131% (all-image AbsRel).
Same direction, same order of magnitude once the different protocol is
accounted for (a uniform draw over [0,45] averages ~22.5 deg).

Two things we have that they do not: a **fisheye** setting where rolling the
picture (rather than the camera) injects black wedges that account for two
thirds of the apparent fall-off, and a **measured** roll distribution from a
real head-mounted device (h17).
