# Breaking the Horizontal Prior — arXiv 2608.00678

**Re-found 2026-09-09** during a hardware outage. **It was not new to the
line** — §02b of the report already cites it, names its four models, and was
designed as a test of its attribution. What this note adds is the paper's
numbers, an explicit statement of what it does and does not cover, and the
observation that the credit present in §02b never reached the summary
sections.

URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2608.00678

## What it claims

A **"Horizontal Prior"**: training images are overwhelmingly captured near
horizontal because of photographic convention, so monocular depth models
degrade under camera roll. Independently establishes the premise this line's
H17.2 established for itself.

## What it measures

* **Models**: Marigold, GenPercept (diffusion), DAv2, DistillAD (ViT
  feedforward). **All single-image.**
* **Data**: DIODE, ScanNet, ETH3D, KITTI, NYUv2 — **all perspective.**
* **Numbers** (DAv2, pooled over five benchmarks):

  | setting | AbsRel | vs horizontal |
  |---|---|---|
  | horizontal 0° | 0.096 | — |
  | shaking [0,15°] | 0.109 | +13.5% |
  | rolling [0,45°] | 0.119 | +23.9% |
  | tipping [0,90°] | 0.127 | +32.3% |

* **Fix**: *ID-Constraint* — rebalanced rotation augmentation plus six
  rotation-invariant auxiliary tasks. Training-based. 0.135 → 0.106 on the
  tipping setting.

## What it does NOT cover

**No fisheye. No egocentric data. No multi-view-pretrained models. No
horizontal-flip or mirror equivariance.** Confirmed by direct reading.

## What this means for this line — honestly

**Corroborated, not novel — and §02b already said so.** *Monocular depth models
are roll-sensitive because of the pretraining orientation distribution* is
theirs. §02b opens by attributing it to them and frames H17.2 as testing whether
the attribution survives a backbone without that long tail. The gap was that
§00's one-page row and §04's recommendations bullet stated it with no source.

**Still this line's own:**

1. **The pretraining-family split.** Their four models are all single-image.
   This line's central roll finding — VGGT and VGGT-Omega are **4–5× less
   roll-sensitive** than the DA3 pair, and more accurate — has no counterpart
   there, and is now a whole-curve result on thirteen recordings (H42).
2. **Fisheye and egocentric.** Their five benchmarks are perspective; this line
   is Aria fisheye with a rim/centre decomposition.
3. **The expected cost against a real roll distribution.** They evaluate at
   synthetic angle ranges. H17.1 + H35 integrate the curve against 60,105
   frames of measured head roll and get 0.46–1.85% — a different and, for
   deployment, more decision-relevant quantity.
4. **A training-free fix.** Their ID-Constraint is a training recipe. H38/H39's
   answer is to fold the known gravity vector into the resampling grid: no
   labels, no training, no model change, 2.65% ± 2.43 on a single-image
   backbone and nothing on a multi-view one.
5. **Mirror equivariance** (H45–H49) is untouched by them.

## The methodological point the search also turned up

"Flip the image, predict, flip the prediction back, take the relative absolute
error" is an **established inconsistency measure** in this literature, not
something this line invented. H45's construction should be described as a
standard probe applied to a new setting, not as a new probe.
