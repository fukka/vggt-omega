# Feedback on the H2 readout-head line (runs 010–013, tickets #29/#30/#31)

Three concerns were raised by the human. Two are upheld and one is refuted.
Everything below was checked against the stored result JSONs, the ADT sequence
metadata, and three new CPU control runs on this Mac; the control scripts are
in the session scratchpad and can be moved into
`autoresearch/experiments/h2-center-safe-adapter/code/` if you want them kept.

Reproduction note for the control runs: no usable torch remains on the Mac, so
run 011's pipeline was reimplemented in numpy against the surviving frozen
DA3-Small caches. The KB4 incidence grid was validated to 7 decimals against
the published `theta_bin_mid_deg` (theta_max 54.826685 vs 54.826683). The
uncorrected baseline differs from the published one (near-rim 1.173 vs 1.023)
because GT is resized with a coarser nearest-index map; the published head
arm reproduces to 0.337 vs the published 0.333, so arm-vs-arm comparisons
below are sound while absolute values are not bit-identical.

---

## 1. UPHELD — the headline percentages are governed by the baseline, not by the head

`after` is close to a constant floor while `before` swings by 4x.

Cross-scene folds (run_012), near-rim AbsRel:

| fold | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| clean_seq131 | 1.271 | 0.324 |
| clean_seq133 | 1.475 | 0.361 |
| clean_seq134 | 1.259 | 0.313 |
| clean_seq135 | 1.544 | 0.340 |
| clean_seq136 | 1.359 | 0.344 |
| decoration_seq132 | 0.353 | 0.287 |

`before` spread 4.4x, `after` spread 1.26x. decoration_seq132's "weak
-18.7%" is not a transfer failure — its baseline was already at the floor the
head converges to everywhere else. Reporting the percentage makes an
easy sequence look like a failure and a badly-aligned sequence look like a
triumph.

Two further findings on where `before` comes from:

- **Most of it is the eval affine, not the rim.** Refitting the per-frame
  `scale_shift` affine on near pixels only (instead of all valid pixels) drops
  seq131's uncorrected near-rim AbsRel from 1.474/1.284 (far-heavy /
  near-heavy frame halves) to 0.263 in both. ~82% of the "1.0-1.5 near-rim
  penalty" is the single global affine being fit mostly on mid/far pixels,
  not a property of the rim. This sharpens the already-recorded lesson
  "under per-frame re-alignment, local corrections move remote cells".
- **decoration_seq132 has a genuinely different composition**: 58.6% of cone
  pixels within 2 m (clean sequences: 30–34%) and 24.6% of cone pixels in the
  near-rim zone (clean: ~15%). Within a single sequence, though, the
  correlation between a frame's <2 m share and its near-rim error is only
  -0.385, so the depth histogram alone does not explain the gap. n=1 on
  decoration; do not build a story on it.

**Also: the per-sequence numbers have far wider error bars than the reported
deltas.** seq131's 28 frames have per-frame near-rim AbsRel from 0.19 to 2.99.
Bootstrapping a random 14-frame split gives a 90% range of **0.879–1.633**.
The published `halves` split's 0.639 sits *below* that range — contiguous
halves are temporally correlated (the wearer is in a different part of the
room), so `halves` is not an independent resample of `even_odd` and the two
should not be read as agreeing or disagreeing evidence.

## 2. UPHELD — "cross-scene" is not cross-scene

From `groundtruth/metadata.json` of the local sequence:
`"scene": "Apartment"`, `"serial": "1WM103600M1292"`, `dataset ADT_2023 v2.0`.
All six sequence names begin `Apartment_release_`, and the `M1292` suffix in
every name is that device serial. The trajectory spans 4.62 x 0.78 x 5.10 m
with a 60.8 m path length.

So run_012's leave-one-out is: **one ~25 m² apartment, one Aria device, six
recording sessions, one of which (decoration) is a redecoration of the same
room.** That is cross-*sequence*, not cross-scene, and it is same-camera by
construction rather than by demonstration.

`cross_scene.py` also hard-assumes one camera: `geometry(train_sd[0].src)`
supplies the cone mask, theta bins and per-patch theta for *every* sequence
including the held-out one, while each `SeqData` installs the backbone with
its own camera. Correct for ADT, silent and wrong for anything else.

## 3. REFUTED — it is not "just a function of FOV and depth"

New matched-capacity ablation, seq131 even/odd, identical training in every
respect except the input (near-rim / near-center AbsRel):

| head input | near rim | near center |
|---|---|---|
| uncorrected | 1.173 | 0.556 |
| sin/cos theta only | 0.708 (−40%) | 0.785 (**+41%**) |
| theta + log predicted depth | 0.779 (−34%) | 0.654 (**+18%**) |
| theta + depth + frozen features | **0.337 (−71%)** | **0.382 (−31%)** |

A same-capacity MLP given only FOV and predicted depth — i.e. a smooth,
continuous version of the H2.1 table — recovers less than half the rim gain
**and reproduces the H2.1 table's center damage**. Only the image features
both double the rim gain and flip the center from damaged to improved.

This is the control the H2.2 protocol should have carried: it compared against
the 48-param *discrete* table, so "the features are doing the work" was
inferred rather than isolated. It now holds on measurement.

**But this refutes only the "trivial radial function" reading of the concern.
It says nothing about whether the features generalize past this apartment** —
they could equally be encoding "ADT apartment appearance". That question is
open and untested.

The `halves` split agrees in direction with a smaller margin — near rim:
uncorrected 0.770, theta only 0.503, theta+depth 0.518, full **0.360**; near
center: uncorrected 0.342, theta only 0.403 (+18%), theta+depth 0.325 (−5%),
full **0.289 (−15%)**. Features still roughly double the rim gain (−53% vs
−33%) and are still the only arm that clearly helps the center, but the gap is
half what even/odd shows — another reason the two splits should not be read as
independent confirmations of each other (item 3).

## Requested changes

1. **Rename the claim** everywhere (findings.md, paper/, dashboard): "one head
   across six sequences of one apartment on one device". Reserve "cross-scene"
   until a second scene exists. If ADT's Office scene is on the box, that is
   the cheapest real second scene.

2. **Lead with absolute `after`, not the percentage.** Near-rim AbsRel 0.33 is
   still a bad number in absolute terms; the -75% headline hides that the head
   converges to the same mediocre floor from every starting point. Report
   `after` first, `before`/delta second.

3. **Put error bars on every per-sequence number.** Bootstrap over test frames
   (frames are the sampling unit). Stop treating `even_odd` and `halves` as two
   independent replications — state explicitly that `halves` is temporally
   correlated, or replace it with a second random split.

4. **Add an alignment-robustness row to the protocol of record.** For every
   before/after table also report: affine frozen from the uncorrected
   prediction, `scale_only`, and affine fit restricted to the eval zone. If the
   near-rim number moves by ~4x across these (as it does on seq131), any claim
   phrased as a rim property must be re-phrased as a rim-under-this-alignment
   property.

5. **Make the aux-only arm a permanent baseline row**, alongside the H2.1
   table, in every head/adapter experiment from here (H5, H6, H7 included). It
   is nearly free and it is the arm that separates "the adapter learned
   something about the image" from "the adapter learned the lens".

6. **Cross-dataset is now the load-bearing experiment, not a nice-to-have.**
   ScanNet++ 3f15 is already loadable in `raytun3r/`, already audited, and is
   RayTun3R's named sequence — but it is a different camera (~170° DSLR
   fisheye), so item 7 blocks it. Run the head there before any further
   within-ADT variant.

7. **Guard the camera assumption in `cross_scene.py`** before any cross-camera
   run: assert every sequence's camera matches the first, or compute theta per
   sequence. Today it would silently produce plausible wrong numbers.

Not disputed and worth keeping: the H2.1-table-vs-feature-head contrast, the
zero-init readout-side design and its pose-safety-by-construction argument,
and the backbone-agnostic result on VGGT-Omega (which, note, shows both a
smaller effect and larger center collateral — consistent with the features,
not the geometry, being the active ingredient).
