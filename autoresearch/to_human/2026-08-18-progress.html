Peripheral-aware fisheye adaptation — day 1 (2026-08-18)

Question: adapt depth backbones to Aria ~110° fisheye with limited finetuning — improve the high-FOV region without hurting the center; understand what the periphery is actually good for (your N1–N4 axes).

TLDR of day 1 (5 runs, all CPU, all on the pre-verified classical harness + DA3-Small): your N3 intuition — "the rim is hard for depth but good for alignment" — is now measured, from three angles, and it came out sharper than the folklore version: the rim's alignment value is real but it is span-under-real-noise, not per-point quality; and the frozen model already leans on the rim for pose (its pose survives deletion of the entire center), while its depth is worst there.

Why this matters for the method we build

  • The Pareto goal (N1) gains a third axis: any adapter that touches rim features risks the pose path that demonstrably depends on them. Every variant must report (center depth, rim depth, pose stability).
  • Design constraint: prefer zero-init, readout-side corrections (RayTun3R-style PE residual, decoder-grid fixes) over rewriting early rim features. "Improve rim depth without disturbing rim-driven alignment" is the new, concrete objective.
  • Negative results that save future work: attention/weighting schemes premised on "rim points are individually more informative" have no basis (H1); theory-only conditioning analyses underestimate the periphery's value ~20× (H1.1's two arms).
